# Sensor technical description

## Sensor Wiring & Physical Connection

The M81190 fluxgate compass is a purely analog sensing device containing
gimbal-stabilized drive and sensing coils. It has no digital processing onboard
and does not output SeaTalk or NMEA datagrams directly.

The compass connects via a 5-core color-coded cable (typically 9 meters long):
* **Red:** Sine/cosine pickup coil positive
* **Green:** Sine/cosine pickup coil positive
* **Yellow:** Coil common / reference
* **Blue:** Drive coil excitation
* **Screen (Shield):** Ground / excitation return

## Coil Resistance Bench Test
Sensor integrity can be verified with a multimeter across the disconnected wire
leads (nominal readings at 20°C):
* **Red to Green:** ~4 Ω (typically 3–6 Ω)
* **Red to Yellow:** ~4 Ω (typically 3–6 Ω)
* **Green to Yellow:** ~8 Ω (typically 6–10 Ω)
* **Blue to Screen (Shield):** ~8 Ω (typically 6–10 Ω)
* **All other wire combinations:** Open circuit (infinite resistance)

# Seatalk1/S2G integration

## Connection into S2G Course Computer

In the legacy Seagram vessel architecture, the M81190 is wired directly into
the dedicated **FLUXGATE COMPASS** terminal block on the **Raymarine S2G
SmartPilot** course computer board:
* Terminals: `RED`, `GRN`, `YEL`, `BLU`, `SCREEN`
* **Direct Wiring Rationale:** As highlighted in [iTC-5.md](iTC-5.md),
  autopilot-critical sensors (compass and rudder feedback) require low-latency,
  sub-millisecond feedback. Wiring the compass straight into the S2G course
  computer guarantees that the steering control loop operates without network
  jitter or external converter latency.
* **Analog-to-Digital & Gyro Stabilization:** The S2G motherboard samples the
  analog coils of the M81190 and fuses heading data with the internal
  **GyroPlus** rate-of-turn yaw gyro. This enables 10 Hz heading stabilization,
  dampening wave-induced yaw and pitch/roll motion.

## SeaTalk1 Heading Output Data

Once digitized and gyro-stabilized, the S2G publishes compass and autopilot
data onto the vessel's 3-wire **SeaTalk1** bus (Red `+12V`, Yellow `Data`,
Screen `0V`).

The S2G transmits the following compass-related SeaTalk1 datagrams:
* **Datagram `9C` (Heading and Rudder Position):**
  * Format: `9C U1 VW RR`
  * Transmits instantaneous heading in degrees and vessel turning direction
    (port/starboard yaw), alongside rudder angle (`RR`). Heading is computed as
    `(U & 0x3) * 90 + (VW & 0x3F) * 2 + (U & 0xC ? (U & 0xC == 0xC ? 2 : 1) :
    0)`. Bit 7 of `U` specifies the turning direction (1 = turning
    right/starboard, 0 = turning left/port).
* **Datagram `84` (Autopilot Status, Heading, and Locked Course):**
  * Format: `84 U6 VW XY 0Z 0M RR SS TT`
  * Broadcasts the current stabilized magnetic compass heading (`U/VW`), locked
    target heading (`XY`), autopilot operating state (`Z`: Standby, Auto, Vane,
    Track), and rudder position (`RR`).
* **Datagram `99` (Compass Variation):**
  * Format: `99 00 XX` (or `99 00 YY`)
  * Broadcasts magnetic variation in degrees when received from GPS or
    configured in system settings.

When bridged to SeaTalkng or NMEA 2000 (via a SeaTalk-to-SeaTalkng converter or
YDNU-02 / YDWG-02 gateway), these datagrams convert into standard PGN 127250
(Vessel Heading) and PGN 127251 (Rate of Turn).

### SeaTalk1 Protocol Specifications & References
Because SeaTalk1 is a proprietary 9-bit serial protocol, its datagram formats
have been comprehensively documented and verified through community reverse
engineering and open-source marine implementations:
* [Thomas Knauf's SeaTalk Technical Reference](
  https://thomasknauf.de/seatalk.htm): The canonical reference specification
  for SeaTalk1 bus electrical characteristics, 9-bit UART frame structure, and
  full datagram breakdown (covering datagrams `84`, `99`, and `9C`).
* [Yacht Devices SeaTalk to NMEA 2000 Conversion Specs](
  https://www.yachtd.com/products/seatalk_bridge.html): Details supported
  conversions from SeaTalk datagrams (`84`, `9C`) to NMEA 2000 PGNs (127250
  Heading, 127245 Rudder).
* [Signal K SeaTalk Parser Specification (`signalk-parser-nmea0183`)](
  https://github.com/SignalK/signalk-parser-nmea0183): Open-source
  implementation parsing raw SeaTalk `$STALK` sentence datagrams into standard
  navigation and autopilot data structures.

## Calibration Process

### Purpose of Calibration

Because the M81190 uses sensitive electromagnetic pickup coils, nearby ferrous
metals, engine blocks, wiring looms, and electrical gear introduce magnetic
deviation (distortion of the local magnetic field). Calibration maps and
compensates for this deviation across all 360 degrees and aligns the sensor
readings with the vessel's true centerline (lubber line).

### Calibration Procedure via ST6000 & S2G

Calibration is performed from the cockpit using the **ST6000** (or ST6000+ /
SmartPilot) control head communicating with the S2G over SeaTalk1:

1. **Enter Calibration Mode:**
   * Power on the system with the autopilot in `STANDBY`.
   * Press and hold the `STANDBY` button for approximately 2 to 4 seconds until
     the screen displays `CAL 1` or the calibration menu.
2. **Linearization & Deviation Correction (Sea Trial):**
   * Motor the boat to a wide, calm area of water away from large vessels,
     docks, and steel structures.
   * On the ST6000, navigate to the `COMPASS CAL` (or `DEV`) screen.
   * Steer the vessel in slow, steady circles (speed 2 to 3 knots, rate of turn
     under 3°/second).
   * Complete at least two full 360° circles (taking a minimum of 2 minutes per
     circle).
   * The S2G detects the rotation, calculates the magnetic deviation curve, and
     beeps when complete. The ST6000 displays the maximum detected deviation
     (e.g., `DEV 4°`). If deviation exceeds 15°, the compass sensor must be
     relocated further away from onboard magnetic sources.
3. **Aligning Heading to Ship's Centerline:**
   * Steer the vessel on a known visual compass heading or steady navigational
     range.
   * Step to the `ALIGN HDG` screen on the ST6000.
   * Use the `+1` / `-1` or `+10` / `-10` keys to adjust the heading reading
     until it matches the ship's steering compass or GPS COG in slack water.
4. **AutoLearn (S2G Gyro Feature):**
   * On the S2G, the `AutoLearn` sea-trial routine can be engaged to allow the
     SmartPilot to automatically adjust steering response, counter-rudder, and
     gyro yaw gains.
5. **Saving Settings:**
   * Press and hold `STANDBY` for 2 seconds to store all calibration values and
     exit to normal operation.

### Where Calibration Data is Stored

The calibration data (deviation correction curve, heading alignment offset, and
AutoLearn gain parameters) is **not stored in the M81190 compass** (which is a
passive analog sensor) and is **not stored in the ST6000 display head**.

Instead, the calibration table is written directly to the **non-volatile EEPROM
memory of the S2G course computer**. This ensures that:
* Heading calibration remains intact even when batteries are switched off.
* Replacing or disconnecting the ST6000 control head does not erase the
  calibration.
* The S2G applies deviation correction in hardware before broadcasting
  stabilized heading over SeaTalk1 and NMEA 0183.

# SeatalkNG(N2K)/EV-1+ACU-100 integration

## Obsolescence and Replacement by EV-1 Sensor Core

In modern Raymarine SeaTalkng / NMEA 2000 Evolution autopilot systems (such as
those built around the **EV-1** and **ACU-100**), the analog **M81190 fluxgate
compass transducer is no longer used and is completely obsolete**.

Unlike the [M81105](M81105.md) rudder reference transducer—which is retained
and wired into the ACU-100—the M81190 has **no connection port on either the
ACU-100 or the EV-1**:
* **Direct Replacement:** The M81190 has been entirely superseded by the
  **Raymarine EV-1 Sensor Core** (part number `E70096`).
* **Solid-State 9-Axis AHRS:** The EV-1 is an all-in-one digital Attitude and
  Heading Reference System containing a 3-axis solid-state digital compass,
  3-axis accelerometers, and 3-axis digital angular rate gyros.
* **Bus Architecture:** Rather than routing analog signal wires to a course
  computer, the EV-1 connects directly into the SeaTalkng / NMEA 2000 backbone
  via a standard SeaTalkng spur cable.
* **Elimination of Compass Calibration Sea Trials:** The EV-1 continuously
  auto-compensates for onboard magnetic deviation while underway. It eliminates
  the need for manual ST6000 circular sea trial routines and manual heading
  alignment, outputting stabilized heading (PGN 127250), rate of turn (PGN
  127251), and vessel attitude/pitch/roll (PGN 127257) directly onto the NMEA
  2000 network at up to 10 Hz.
