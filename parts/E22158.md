# Architecture & Network Integration

The E22158 acts as an inline backbone segment or a dedicated 5-way bridge block
connecting legacy SeaTalk1 equipment to SeaTalkng / NMEA 2000 backbones.

## Bridging SeaTalk1 to Modern N2K Networks

The converter translates bidirectional data streams between SeaTalk1 datagrams
and standard NMEA 2000 PGNs:
* **Depth, Speed, Temperature:** ST60 TriData, depth, and speed instruments
  broadcast onto SeaTalk1 and translate to standard PGNs (128267, 128259,
  130310/130311).
* **Wind:** Apparent and true wind angle/speed from ST60 Wind translate into
  PGN 130306.
* **Heading & Rudder:** Heading datagrams and rudder reference positions from
  autopilot course computers (such as S2G or ST600x) are converted to PGN
  127250 (Vessel Heading) and PGN 127245 (Rudder).
* **GPS & Navigation:** Position, SOG, COG, and waypoint/cross-track error
  translate seamlessly across both domains.
* **Firmware Note:** Ensure the converter is updated to firmware v1.21 or v2.08
  via a Raymarine MFD or software update tool for complete autopilot datagram
  and sentence bridging support.

## Power Isolation & Bus Power Rules

* **Single Power Source Rule:** A SeaTalkng / NMEA 2000 network must only be
  powered from a single 12 V DC injection point.
* **SeaTalk1 Bus Powering:** The E22158 passes 12 V DC from its SeaTalkng
  backbone/spur input out to the red conductor of the SeaTalk1 yellow spur
  port. If an existing SeaTalk1 bus already receives 12 V power from an
  autopilot course computer (e.g., S2G Course Computer SeaTalk terminals), the
  red power wire in the SeaTalk1 connecting cable must be disconnected and
  insulated to avoid conflicting dual power feeds or ground loops.

# Integration with OpenPlotter via Yacht Devices Gateways

Coupled with a Yacht Devices gateway, the E22158 gives OpenPlotter and Signal K
full access to legacy SeaTalk1 sensor data without needing custom SeaTalk1 GPIO
opto-isolator circuits.

## With YDNU-02 (USB Gateway)

1. **Physical Setup:**
   * Insert the E22158 into the SeaTalkng backbone.
   * Connect the SeaTalk1 devices into the yellow converter port using the
     supplied adapter cable.
   * Connect the YDNU-02 gateway spur drop into a white SeaTalkng spur port (or
     via a SeaTalkng-to-DeviceNet adapter cable).
   * Plug the YDNU-02 USB-A connector into the Raspberry Pi running
     OpenPlotter.
2. **OpenPlotter Configuration:**
   * Open the **OpenPlotter CAN Bus app** (or Serial app).
   * Detect the serial port (`/dev/ttyACM*`) assigned to the YDNU-02.
   * Attach it as a `slcand` CAN socket interface (`can0`).
   * In Signal K, enable the NMEA 2000 (`can0`) provider. All SeaTalk1 data
     translated by the E22158 appears directly as standard Signal K paths
     (`environment.wind.*`, `navigation.speedThroughWater`,
     `steering.rudderAngle`, etc.).

## With YDWG-02 (Wi-Fi Gateway)

1. **Physical Setup:**
   * Connect the E22158 converter to the SeaTalk1 and SeaTalkng bus.
   * Connect the YDWG-02 to the SeaTalkng backbone spur.
2. **OpenPlotter Configuration:**
   * Join the Raspberry Pi to the YDWG-02 Wi-Fi network (or connect YDWG-02 to
     the boat's central router in client mode).
   * In the **Signal K Data Connections** dashboard, add a connection with type
     **TCP Client** (or **UDP**), point to the gateway's IP address and port
     (default port 1456 or 2304), and set the data format to **Yacht Devices
     RAW** or **NMEA 0183**.
   * Signal K will ingest the continuous stream containing both modern N2K data
     and legacy SeaTalk1 data bridged by the E22158.
