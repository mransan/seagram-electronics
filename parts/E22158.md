## Bridging SeaTalk1 to Modern N2K Networks

The converter translates bidirectional data streams between SeaTalk1 datagrams
and standard NMEA 2000 PGNs:
* **Depth, Speed, Temperature:** ST60 TriData, depth, and speed instruments
  broadcast onto SeaTalk1 and translate to standard PGNs (128267, 128259,
  130310/130311).
* **Wind:** Apparent and true wind angle/speed from ST60 Wind translate into
  PGN 130306.
* **Heading & Rudder:** Heading datagrams and rudder reference positions from
  autopilot course computers (such as S2G or ST600x) are converted to PGN
  127250 (Vessel Heading) and PGN 127245 (Rudder).
* **GPS & Navigation:** Position, SOG, COG, and waypoint/cross-track error
  translate seamlessly across both domains.
* **Firmware Note:** Ensure the converter is updated to firmware v1.21 or v2.08
  via a Raymarine MFD or software update tool for complete autopilot datagram
  and sentence bridging support.

## Power Architecture & Isolation Rules

The E22158 internally bridges the 12 V DC power rail between the SeaTalkng bus
and the SeaTalk1 yellow spur port.

### Single Power Source Rule
A combined SeaTalkng / SeaTalk1 network must receive 12 V DC operating power
from **only one** source to avoid destructive dual-power feeds, blown fuses, or
ground loop interference.

### Powering Direction Options & Current Limitations

1. **Option A (Standard): SeaTalkng Powers SeaTalk1**
   * Power is injected into the SeaTalkng backbone (protected by a 5 A fuse or
     breaker at the DC distribution panel).
   * The converter passes up to **950 mA peak supply current** to legacy
     SeaTalk1 devices attached to its yellow spur port.
   * If the legacy SeaTalk1 bus has its own power feed (e.g., from an autopilot
     course computer like an S2G or dedicated breaker), that feed must be
     disconnected (see *Insulating the Red Power Conductor* below).

2. **Option B: SeaTalk1 Powers SeaTalkng Backbone**
   * If most instruments on the vessel are legacy SeaTalk1 and the SeaTalk1 bus
     is already powered (e.g., via the S2G Course Computer SeaTalk terminals),
     the 12 V DC power can flow backward across the E22158 converter onto the
     SeaTalkng / N2K backbone.
   * In this setup, do **not** connect the SeaTalkng power cable.
   * **Limitation & Warning:** The internal traces and pass-through circuitry
     of the converter block are rated for a **maximum of 950 mA to 1 A total
     spur current**. In addition, the internal power supply of legacy autopilot
     computers (such as the S2G SeaTalk output) is typically rated for only
     around 1.5 A to 2 A total across all connected SeaTalk displays.
     Therefore:
     * This setup is only suitable for very minimal N2K networks consisting of
       1 or 2 low-power N2K devices (such as a single YDNU-02 USB gateway [LEN
       1 / ~30 mA] or YDWG-02 Wi-Fi gateway [LEN 1-2 / ~40 mA]).
     * It cannot support multi-drop N2K backbones containing high-draw MFD
       displays, radar scanners, or multiple N2K instruments. For larger
       setups, standard Option A (independent fused SeaTalkng backbone power
       with SeaTalk1 red wire isolated) must be used.

### Procedure for Insulating the Red Power Conductor

When the SeaTalkng backbone is powered separately from an already-powered
SeaTalk1 network, the red (+12V) conductor bridging the two networks must be
electrically isolated:

1. **Option 1 (Non-destructive - Course Computer Terminal):** If the SeaTalk1
   cable runs between the E22158 converter and the screw terminals of an
   autopilot course computer (such as an S2G), simply loosen the screw terminal
   labeled **SeaTalk RED (+12V)** on the course computer, remove the red wire
   conductor, fold it back, cover the bare copper end with heat-shrink tubing
   or quality electrical tape, and secure it with a cable tie. Leave Yellow
   (Data) and Screen/Black (Ground) securely connected.
2. **Option 2 (Non-destructive - Pin Extraction):** On standard 3-pin flat
   SeaTalk1 molded connectors, use a small pick or needle to gently release the
   locking tab of the center pin (Pin 2, Red), slide the female socket contact
   out of the housing, and apply heat-shrink tubing over the contact.
3. **Option 3 (Cable Splice):** If using the 400 mm SeaTalk1-to-SeaTalkng spur
   cable (Raymarine A06047) connected to a junction block, carefully slit the
   outer sheath, snip only the **Red** wire conductor, stagger the cut ends,
   and cap both exposed ends with adhesive-lined marine heat-shrink tubing to
   prevent any short circuits against the ground/shield or data wires.

# Integration with OpenPlotter via Yacht Devices Gateways

The E22158 acts as a protocol bridge rather than an end-node host interface. To
connect the bridged network to OpenPlotter / Signal K on a Raspberry Pi or
laptop, connect a Yacht Devices NMEA 2000 gateway to an open white spur port on
the SeaTalkng backbone:
* Use [`YDNU-02`](YDNU-02.md) for direct USB connection to the Raspberry Pi
  (mapped via OpenPlotter CAN Bus / `slcand` to `can0`).
* Use [`YDWG-02`](YDWG-02.md) for wireless TCP/UDP connection over Wi-Fi
  (configured in Signal K Data Connections).

Refer to the respective device documentation in this folder for complete
physical cabling and software configuration steps.
