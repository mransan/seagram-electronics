# Connection & Integration

## Sensor Wiring & Physical Connection

The M81105 is a purely analog sensing transducer that houses a sealed,
high-precision marine potentiometer (nominal total resistance of 5 kΩ). It has
no microprocessor, digital circuitry, or native SeaTalk/NMEA output.

The unit connects via an integrated 4-wire shielded cable (10 meters / 33
feet):
* **Red:** Potentiometer positive supply rail (+5V DC excitation from course
  computer)
* **Green:** Potentiometer negative / ground return (0V)
* **Blue:** Potentiometer wiper (variable analog output voltage proportional to
  rudder angle)
* **Screen (Shield / Drain):** Bare drain wire connected to RF ground to shield
  against electrical interference

### Potentiometer Resistance Bench Test
Sensor health and wiper integrity can be verified with a multimeter when the
leads are disconnected from the course computer:
* **Red to Green:** Constant ~5 kΩ (±10%), steady across the full range of
  rudder movement.
* **Blue to Red:** ~2.5 kΩ with rudder centered; smoothly ranges between ~1.6
  kΩ and ~3.3 kΩ when swung lock-to-lock.
* **Blue to Green:** ~2.5 kΩ with rudder centered; smoothly ranges inversely
  between ~3.3 kΩ and ~1.6 kΩ lock-to-lock.
* **Wiper Sweep Verification:** As the wheel turns slowly from stop to stop,
  the resistance must change progressively without open-circuit spikes, sudden
  dropouts, or erratic jumps (which indicate worn dead spots on the resistive
  track).

## Connection into S2G Course Computer

In the Seagram vessel architecture, the M81105 rudder transducer is wired
directly into the dedicated 4-pin **RUDDER** terminal block on the **Raymarine
S2G SmartPilot** course computer motherboard:
* Terminal labels: `RED`, `BLU`, `GRN`, `SCREEN`
* **Direct Wiring Rationale:** As documented in [iTC-5.md](iTC-5.md),
  autopilot-critical sensors (rudder reference and fluxgate compass) require
  real-time, zero-latency feedback. Wiring the rudder reference directly into
  the S2G hardware ensures the autopilot steering loop receives instantaneous
  analog angle feedback without bus traffic latency or network converter
  delays.
* **Analog-to-Digital Conversion:** The S2G course computer supplies a steady
  regulated voltage across the red and green legs and samples the variable
  voltage from the blue wiper line via an onboard Analog-to-Digital Converter
  (ADC). The digitized voltage is converted into a calibrated rudder angle in
  degrees.
* **Phase Inversion (Reversal):** If the physical linkage arm orientation
  causes the rudder angle indicator to move opposite to the actual wheel turn
  (e.g., turning the helm to starboard shows port deflection), swapping the
  `RED` and `GRN` wires at the S2G terminal block inverts the electrical
  polarity and corrects the sensed direction.

## SeaTalk1 Rudder Output Data

Once digitized and processed by the S2G course computer, the rudder angle data
is published onto the vessel's 3-wire **SeaTalk1** bus (Red `+12V`, Yellow
`Data`, Screen `0V`).

The S2G broadcasts rudder position across two primary SeaTalk1 datagrams:
* **Datagram `9C` (Compass Heading and Rudder Position):**
  * Format: `9C U1 VW RR`
  * Byte `RR`: Signed 8-bit integer representing rudder position in degrees.
    Positive values denote starboard rudder (e.g., `0x02` = 2° starboard);
    two's complement negative values denote port rudder (e.g., `0xFE` = -2°
    port). Transmitted at high repetition rates (multiple times per second)
    alongside vessel heading (`U1 VW`) for immediate display and steering
    control.
* **Datagram `84` (Autopilot Status, Heading, and Rudder Position):**
  * Format: `84 U6 VW XY 0Z 0M RR SS TT`
  * Byte `RR`: Transmits instantaneous rudder angle in degrees (same signed
    representation) alongside the locked course (`XY`), autopilot operating
    mode (`0Z`: Standby, Auto, Track, Wind Vane), and compass heading.

When bridged to SeaTalkng / NMEA 2000 through a SeaTalk-to-SeaTalkng converter
or Yacht Devices gateway ([YDNU-02](YDNU-02.md) / [YDWG-02](YDWG-02.md)), these
SeaTalk1 datagrams translate into standard **PGN 127245** (Rudder).

### SeaTalk1 Protocol Specifications & References

# Calibration Process

## Purpose of Calibration
Mechanical tolerances, mounting bracket variations, and minor offsets in the
linkage tiller pin mean that the potentiometer's electrical midpoint rarely
aligns perfectly with the rudder's physical amidships (zero-degree) position.
Furthermore, the autopilot course computer must know the vessel's physical
rudder stops (hard-over port and hard-over starboard) to prevent the drive unit
from straining against mechanical stops.

Calibration accomplishes two key objectives:
1. **Rudder Centering (Alignment):** Zeroing the sensed angle when the rudder
   is physically centered amidships.
2. **Rudder Limits:** Defining the maximum travel angle so the autopilot
   software stops driving the rudder just inside the mechanical steering stops.

## Calibration Procedure via ST6000 & S2G
Rudder calibration is configured dockside using the **ST6000** (or compatible
ST6001 / ST6002 SmartPilot) control head connected to the S2G:

1. **Enter Dealer Calibration:**
   * Power on the autopilot system with the control head in `STANDBY` mode.
   * Press and hold `STANDBY` for approximately 2 seconds until the display
     enters calibration mode (`CAL ?`).
   * Press `DISP` until `CAL ?` is displayed, then press `AUTO` to display
     `DEALER CAL`.
   * Press the `-1` and `+1` buttons simultaneously to enter Dealer
     Calibration.
2. **Rudder Zero Alignment (`ALIGN RUDDER`):**
   * Manually align the physical helm wheel/tiller so the rudder is visually
     and mechanically straight amidships (0°).
   * Cycle `DISP` to the `ALIGN RUDDER` screen.
   * Observe the digital rudder bar indicator on the ST6000. Use the `-1`,
     `+1`, `-10`, or `+10` buttons to adjust the displayed offset until the
     rudder bar reads exactly `0°`.
3. **Set Rudder Limits (`RUDDER LIMIT`):**
   * Cycle `DISP` to the `RUDDER LIMIT` screen.
   * Turn the helm hard-over to port and hard-over to starboard to observe the
     maximum mechanical deflection angle (typically 30° to 35°).
   * Set the electronic rudder limit value to roughly 2° to 3° *less* than the
     maximum physical travel. This prevents the autopilot motor or hydraulic
     pump from driving against structural end stops.
4. **Save and Exit:**
   * Press and hold `STANDBY` (or `DISP`) for 2 seconds to permanently save the
     calibrated parameters into memory and exit to normal `STANDBY` operation.

## Where Calibration Data is Stored
The calibration settings (rudder zero offset and rudder travel limits) are
**not stored in the M81105 sensor** (which is a passive analog transducer) and
are **not stored in the ST6000 control head**.

All calibration values are written directly to the **non-volatile EEPROM memory
of the S2G course computer**:
* Calibration survives main battery power disconnects and winter layup.
* Disconnecting or swapping out the ST6000 control head does not affect the
  calibrated rudder alignment.
* The S2G applies the zero offset and limit bounds to the raw ADC voltage
  before driving the steering actuators and before transmitting rudder
  datagrams (`9C` and `84`) onto the SeaTalk1 bus.
